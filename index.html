The map of the MNCS project family

Machine-native systems should make authority, evidence, and uncertainty explicit.

MNCS Atlas is the lightweight family-level guide to the Machine-Native Complexity Standard research ecosystem: what it is, how its projects fit together, where authority lives, and how a new human or agent can contribute without reconstructing the architecture from ten separate READMEs.

Core boundary

Capability is not authority.

A model can propose. A tool can execute. A verifier can measure. A learning system can remember. None of those roles silently grants the right to promote its own result into governing truth.

FAIL>UNKNOWN>PASS

What is MNCS?

A research program for accepting machine-produced software through bounded evidence.

The Machine-Native Complexity Standard (MNCS) is an open experimental standard for accepting generated or machine-optimized implementations through bounded evidence. MNCDS is its separate development-process specification.

The wider project family tests what happens when contracts, evidence, semantic identity, authority, execution, coordination, and learning are designed for machine participation from the beginning—while remaining inspectable by humans.

Atlas is non-normative. Detailed project documentation and the standards repository remain authoritative for their own domains.

Representative end-to-end flow

A successful step does not automatically promote the next layer. Execution success is not verifier success; verifier success is not independent evaluation.

  1. 1
    Request

    Human or agent states a bounded goal.

  2. 2
    Orchestrate

    Local harness or Forge chooses a declared action and providers.

  3. 3
    Execute

    Fabric admits and runs an exact-target workload, recording execution evidence.

  4. 4
    Observe

    Providers, compilers, or verifiers emit scoped observations.

  5. 5
    Record

    Evidence, lineage, and status are kept explicit; gaps stay UNKNOWN.

  6. 6
    Share & learn

    Commons may publish scoped knowledge; RAVEL/MNEL may update strategy under governance.

  7. 7
    Evaluate

    MNCS/MNCDS validators judge only the claims they are authorized to judge.

01

Execution is not conformance

Fabric can prove that an identified bounded execution occurred under its recorded conditions. That does not make the workload formally MNCS-conformant.

02

Coordination is not command

Commons can preserve claims, evidence, work requests, disputes, and decisions without becoming an unrestricted instruction channel or source of truth.

03

Learning remains governed

RAVEL and MNEL can improve strategy from validated experience, but their confidence cannot turn unsupported evidence into PASS.

04

Tools do not self-promote

Forge, agents, providers, and candidates can create evidence and proposals. Selection and governing status remain outside the candidate's control.

Shared language

A small vocabulary prevents large architectural misunderstandings.

Family terminology is intentionally conservative. The full index lives in docs/TERMINOLOGY.md.

Open the terminology index ↗
Authority
The bounded right to make a particular decision or claim. Capability is not authority.
Evidence
Identified observations or records supporting a bounded claim, with provenance, scope, and freshness.
UNKNOWN
An intentional result for missing, stale, unsupported, unavailable, or insufficient evidence—not an inconvenience to hide.
Provider
A replaceable compiler, analyzer, benchmark, verifier, runtime harness, or other capability used to produce observations.
Worker
An identified execution participant. Availability does not imply independence, trust, or suitability for every claim.

For new contributors & agents

Start with the map. Then read the owning project.

The fastest safe path into MNCS is not to read every repository. It is to identify the authority and responsibility boundary first.

  1. 1
    Orient

    Read Atlas architecture and terminology so family names mean the same thing.

  2. 2
    Locate ownership

    Find the repository that owns the behavior you want to change.

  3. 3
    Read locally

    Use that project's README, specs, architecture docs, tests, and public interfaces as the implementation authority.

  4. 4
    Preserve boundaries

    Do not turn transport into trust, execution into conformance, publication into acceptance, or learning into authority.

  5. 5
    Contribute evidence

    Prefer narrow changes, explicit uncertainty, reproducible results, and versioned public contracts.
