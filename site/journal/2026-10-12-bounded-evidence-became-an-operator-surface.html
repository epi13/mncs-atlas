Bounded evidence became an operator surface

Between October 6 and October 12, language-runtime pressure and Control evidence work made campaign state, reload, recovery, and bounded reads more inspectable, while negative paths remained explicit.

Machine-maintained entry

This entry is machine-maintained: it was synthesized by the Atlas Journal Maintainer from inspectable MNCS project evidence. It is a dated developmental record, not a specification, acceptance decision, or substitute for owning-repository documentation.

Between October 6 and October 12, language-runtime pressure and Control evidence work made campaign state, reload, recovery, and bounded reads more inspectable, while negative paths remained explicit.

Language-runtime pressure moved below the badge layer

The interval included many routine language badge refreshes, which are omitted here. The material language records instead concern bounded compiler capacity and data movement: the Cranelift arena was raised for project and ingestion paths, source-span scaling was recorded, bytecode payloads were streamed while reusing SSA validation, and validated SSA inputs were reused across typed calls. These changes describe pressure at the compiler/runtime boundary rather than a new family-wide result.

The collection also contains lower-confidence records around HIR prelude reuse, call indexing, relocation repair, syntax-span indexing, and bounded process-environment values. They are kept as background signals rather than treated as settled conclusions.

Control exposed bounded evidence and campaign continuation

The strongest thread in the interval is a set of Control surfaces around evidence rather than an unbounded inspection API. The records expose authenticated projection-evidence reads, authenticated campaign-state inspection, a bounded campaign evidence ledger, bounded projection invocation, protected artifact reads, paged evidence over Environment RPC, Store readback, and projection-evidence binding. They also verify project-scoped continuation and repair the campaign-charter evidence window. Taken together, these records make campaign context addressable in smaller, inspectable pieces.

The related continuation records include protected compiler inventory, project-scoped campaign selection, checkpoint and capsule proofs, and Environment entry mapping. The journal records the surfaces and their evidence labels; it does not infer the contents of a live Control session that was not supplied to the maintainer.

Reload and recovery became part of the record

Control's evidence records also cover Environment source reload, automatic reload, canonical response recovery, and service-termination recovery. Read with the protected Store and continuation entries, the emphasis is on making ordinary failure and restart boundaries visible in the same record flow as successful reads. This gives the operator stack a more useful developmental shape: a recovery is something to record and inspect, not something to silently assume.

Several lower-confidence records extend the same direction through workspace entry capsules, post-commit restart faults, scoped campaign grants, resume retries after RPC restart, and checkpoint response-window faults. They remain supporting evidence, not a substitute for a supplied operator-context bundle.

Negative paths stayed explicit

The interval does not contain only successful evidence. Control preserved bounded Forge MCP failure details and unknown Forge probe outcomes instead of flattening them into a positive result. The debug record skips native-call tests unless the launcher supports the call, which is a visible execution limitation. These entries narrow what can be inferred from the surrounding projection and recovery work: a bounded read, a recovery record, or a compiler change is not by itself proof that every dependent path is available.

The negative records are part of the development history. They identify where the operator surface still depends on launcher capabilities, bounded probes, and explicit failure handling.

Collection boundaries

The maintainer inspected the bounded owning-repository set and read six prior journal entries for continuity. The bundle did not include an MNCS Control journal-context bundle, an experiment snapshot or public experiment interface, or a configured Commons public interface. The owning-repository map also reported mncs-test as empty for this collection; that is a collection result, not evidence that the project had no development. These limits are preserved rather than filled with inference.
